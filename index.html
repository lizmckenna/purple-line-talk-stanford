Why does so much mobilization leave so little lasting capacity behind — and what sets apart the rare efforts that build it?

Why This Matters for Civil Society & Sustainability

Climate is the paradigm case of a fight that needs durable power, not episodic surges — the work outlasts any single mobilization.

  • The crowd is not the capacity. The largest climate marches and strikes produce mostly one-and-done turnout. Headcounts measure a moment; they do not measure the organization left behind.
  • Two things have to be true, not one. Sustained power needs participation that is both active (people exercising agency, not just attending) and relational (tied to others they develop). Postcard-writing and solo phone-banking can be highly active yet build no collective capacity — agency without relationship.
  • The question I'll answer: what converts a surge of activity into lasting capacity? The short version is leadership density — the thin stratum who stay, are developed, and go on to develop others.
  • Measure what you want to build. Organizations should track that stratum and, above all, log who develops whom — the most diagnostic datum of a movement's relational substrate.

Cf. Fisher (2024), Saving Ourselves: From Climate Shocks to Climate Action — climate action turns on sustained civic capacity, not shocks alone.

The Participation-Power Gap: What We Don't Know

  • Contentious politics tradition (Tarrow & Tilly; DOCA): relies on protest event counts; lacks a view into internal organizational structure
  • Civic engagement tradition (Verba, Schlozman, Brady): individual surveys
  • Unobserved: how respondents are relationally connected, longitudinal participation, and internal org structure
  • Movement and org theory exchange has enriched both traditions (Davis, McAdam, Scott & Zald 2005; Walker 2012; King and Soule 2007; Soule et al. 2023) yet the integration remains incomplete
  • An understanding of the transmission mechanisms between inputs (participation) and outputs (influence) remains ripe for exploration

But the definition does real work. Tighten it — from "shows up a lot" to "spans organizing and mobilizing, and clusters into a team" — and the leaderboard reshuffles.

Civic OH leads on the loose threshold but collapses under the strict one — its loyal returners are single-mode. The orgs that hold are the ones whose returners span both modes and sit in teams.

16b

So what were the single-mode returners doing? Civic OH's loyal leaders are an organizing silo — 96% of their activity is organizing, they almost never cross into mobilizing. Civic Federation NC is the mirror image: a mobilizing funnel.

The hybrid leader — fluent in both modes — is what FaithMN and Rural Civic NC build at scale (84–88%). It is the thing a single-mode org, however loyal its base, never produces.

Recruitment is depth-1: a tiny core of recruiters throws off stars of one-and-done recruits. Chains that keep going — cascades — are vanishingly rare.

73 people account for every recorded recruit; only 12 are cascade recruiters. Just 2% of ties are second-generation.

19

Volume is not the same as reproduction. One leader recruited 67 people — none of whom recruited anyone. Another recruited 7 — and the tree kept growing.

The hub's recruits retained at 0.00 over 90 days. The cascade's recruits retained at 0.50 — against an org average of 0.21. Structure, not headcount, is what reproduces.

19b

A team = people who keep showing up to the same events together. Each color is a distinct team. The four strongest orgs carry many overlapping teams; their teamed core is a quarter of everyone who walked in.

Slide down the gradient and the teams thin out — from 25% teamed to near zero. That share tracks which organizations turned 2024's mobilization into durable wins.

Proposed Mechanisms

Based on companion qualitative research: 2 years of fieldwork; 50+ in-depth interviews

  1. 1

    Culture of agency

    Agency develops not through repetition but through proximity to others acting agentically. It requires sufficient density to spread.

  2. 2

    Relational bandwidth

    Each leader sustains finite accountability relationships; below threshold, orgs bleed members after defeats

  3. 3

    Signal credibility to power

    Officials distinguish organized relationships from mobilized turnout; threshold marks when density becomes legible as durable threat

  4. 4

    Redundancy and succession

    Below threshold: org is 1-2 people deep; above threshold: survives burnout and targeting

  5. 5

    Internal knowledge transmission

    Tacit knowledge of local power lives in people; above threshold, organizational learning compounds over cycles

  6. 6

    Boundary-spanning capacity

    Statewide wins require coordinating across nodes (>200 may reflect minimum infrastructure to span territory)

Three Theoretical Conversations

Mass Mobilization & Thresholds

Core Insight

Participation scale associated with regime-level disruption (the 3.5% rule) (Chenoweth & Stephan 2011; Chenoweth 2020: 47% decline in success rate since 2010)

Empirical Domain

Maximalist campaigns, autocratic overthrow

Purple Line Extension

Incremental policy influence at state and local level

Organizing vs. Mobilizing

Core Insight

Depth over breadth (Han 2014; McAlevey 2016)

Empirical Domain

Organizational civic depth

Purple Line Extension

Empirical operationalization of the distinction; validated thresholds from longitudinal data

Exit, Voice, and Loyalty

Core Insight

Exit is default; voice requires investment (Hirschman 1970)

Empirical Domain

Individual disposition toward voice or exit

Purple Line Extension

Without leadership density, exit is structurally unavoidable

Limitations

  • Organizations self-select into data agreements; complete logs exist for 18 of 38 partners
  • Data entry in the CRM is a non-random, two-sided undercount — both organizing and mobilizing events lose records
  • Org-level thresholds rest on a small org-N: suggestive. The defensible center is the individual-level mechanism
  • Recruitment-network findings are scoped to the orgs that log "recruited-by" ties
  • Observational, single cycle: org, cohort, and event-access controls reduce but do not eliminate selection
23

Future Directions

  • What builds the purple line?
    • Companion paper (McKenna, Ganz, Lin) shows organizing workshops produce measurable shifts in collective agency
  • Two axes, not one: separating active participation (agency) from relational participation (ties to others) — postcard-writing can be active yet build nothing
  • Why are thresholds where they are? Relational bandwidth hypothesis requires direct testing
  • Democratic backsliding: Does the redundancy mechanism become primary under authoritarian pressure?
